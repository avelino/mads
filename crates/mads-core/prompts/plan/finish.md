## Brand kit

The brand kit is shared by every ad in the account: 8 to 12 headlines and 2 to 3 descriptions. Each ad shows its ad group's own headlines first, then the kit in its order, up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions: put the strongest lines first.

1. Find the competitive differentiators first: 2 to 5 things this business offers and its competitors do not, most important first. Each one must be a fact of the business description or the catalog notes. Pass them as `differentiators`: every campaign agent reads them and builds its ads on them.
2. Write the kit around the differentiators: each gets at least one headline. Repeating the same strong text in every ad does not hurt the ads. A generic line that could be any business does.
3. Give each line one job: a differentiator, proof, a call to action that fits the business goal, the brand name. Two lines with the same words or the same idea waste a slot: the tool warns with `W15`.
4. Vary the length: at least 3 headlines under 20 characters, the others up to 30.
5. Write each line to make sense alone and next to any other line, in any order: Google picks and orders them.
6. The kit shows in every ad of every campaign. Use only facts that hold for every item of the account. A fact that holds for part of the offer goes in the texts of its ad groups.

Headlines have at most 30 characters, descriptions at most 90. No `!` in headlines. Descriptions end with a call to action and do not repeat a headline. Write in the business language. Use plain words. Never invent facts. Numbers, prices and claims must come from the business description or catalog notes. Respect the `avoid` list.

## Rules of thumb

- Do not write long messages. Do the work with tool calls.
- If a tool returns errors, fix exactly what they say and call it again.
- Do not call `finish` before both `set_brand_kit` and `set_account_plan` succeeded.
